The solution

antigen — hijack · sweep · defuse · prove

Antigen sweeps every entity for injection payloads, defuses each one in the graph, and proves the cure by re-running the exact agent it hijacked.

Most DataHub agents read the graph and trust its text. Antigen asks "what if the text is lying?" — and writes the answer back into the graph.
Solution Antigen turns the write-back into the product. It doesn't just detect — it rewrites the poisoned description in place, quarantine-tags the entity, and stamps a tamper-evident hash. Be precise on the rewrite: on the seeded corpus it excises just the injected span. On a catalog it did not seed there is no fixture, so the default is to replace the whole field with an inert quarantine banner — or you opt into --excise-span, which cuts the enclosing sentence — it over-removes on purpose, because detect returns the earliest rule match rather than the payload, so a literal span cut would leave the second clause of a two-clause payload behind. It still falls back to quarantine whenever the survivor trips the detector. Fail-safe, not guaranteed lossless — which is why cure is dry-run by default. The cure lives in the same catalog every agent already reads.

Why it holds up under scrutiny

DETECTOR

A scored rule, not an ML model, not a keyword grep.

Flags a field only on the co-occurrence of a reader-directed imperative AND an agent-action object. Legit prose trips at most one — "ignore null values", "drop_flag column" — so it stays clean. Measured on 38,031 public catalog descriptions we didn't write: 24 flags, 0.063%, all 24 false positives.

UNICODE

Zero-width pre-pass

Strips Cf-category chars on raw text first — NFKC alone would miss them.

ENCODING

Base64 / hex aware

Post-cure gate asserts the payload is gone in every encoding.

CI

Standing control

scan --fail-on-hit fails the build before an agent reads a new injection.

FAIL-SAFE

One-action revert

Nothing deleted — pre-cure text lives in DataHub's aspect history. Fail-safe, not lossless.

Key capabilities The detector is auditable in five minutes and runs in CI with zero dependencies. The subtle win is the zero-width pre-pass — most people assume NFKC removes those characters; it doesn't. On false positives, take the measured number, not our gauntlet: 24 flags in 38,031 public catalog descriptions we did not write, all 24 adjudicated false positives. The number that should change your behaviour is conditional — 0.003% under 200 characters, 4.663% over 2,000 — so it is the long curated descriptions at risk, and the honest caveat is that public-portal text is a proxy: a real enterprise catalog is plausibly worse, not better. Then the part I would lead with if asked what the study is worth: we pointed excise-span at those same 24 real descriptions and it excised one of them, quarantining the other 23 and destroying forty-two thousand characters of real documentation. Structural cause — the span locator got a hand-picked four-rule tuple, and 23 of the 24 flag on data-exfiltration alone, which was not in it. Our demo corpus hid it completely. Every scoring rule now contributes its own match, and it is 23 of 24 excised with 33,411 characters preserved. One still quarantines, and we say so. The study paid for itself by breaking our own cure before a user found it.
